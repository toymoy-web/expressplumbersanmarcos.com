Repiping & Whole-Home Pipe Replacement in San Marcos, CA

Repiping replaces a home's aging or failing water lines rather than patching leak after leak in the same worn-out system. It's a bigger job than a single repair, but for a home with recurring leaks on old galvanized or copper, it's often the point where continuing to patch stops making financial sense.

How We Diagnose the Problem

The first question is always what's actually in the walls: galvanized steel, copper, or an older generation of plastic pipe, and how old it is relative to its typical service life. Homes in San Marcos's more established neighborhoods are more likely to still have original galvanized or copper runs, which corrode and develop pinhole leaks from the inside, often in more than one spot at a time, which is the real signal that a repipe, not another spot repair, is the right call.

Signs You Need This Service

  • More than one pinhole leak on the same supply line within a year or two
  • Discolored or rust-tinted water, especially right after the water's been off
  • Noticeably low water pressure that's gradually worsened over months or years
  • Visible corrosion or greenish staining on exposed copper fittings
  • A home built before the 1980s that's never had its original supply lines replaced

Repiping & Whole-Home Pipe Replacement Pricing & What Affects It

Whole-home repiping costs more up front than a single-point repair, but a home with repeated leaks on the same aging line is usually already paying for that cost in smaller pieces over time, repair calls, water damage from slow leaks, and the eventual repipe anyway. We walk through the real cost comparison between continuing to patch and repiping now, including how many access points are needed and whether drywall or slab work is involved.

Whole-House vs. Partial Repipe

Not every repipe has to be whole-house. If failures are concentrated in one section, say, the original galvanized line feeding one wing of an older home while the rest was already updated, a partial repipe can address the actual problem area without the cost or disruption of redoing lines that are still sound. That distinction gets made during the inspection, not assumed up front.

Minimizing Wall and Drywall Damage

Modern repiping techniques generally route new PEX lines through existing wall cavities and attic or crawlspace access wherever possible, which limits how much drywall needs to be opened compared to older rigid-pipe methods. Where cuts are unavoidable, they're planned to be as few and as targeted as the actual routing allows, and patched as part of the job rather than left for the homeowner.

Why Galvanized Pipe Specifically Is a Repipe Trigger

Galvanized steel pipe corrodes from the inside out, gradually narrowing the interior diameter with rust and mineral buildup, which is why a galvanized-plumbed home often shows a slow, years-long decline in water pressure even before an actual leak appears. By the time a leak does show up, the rest of the same-age line is usually close behind it.

PEX as the Modern Standard

Most repipes today use PEX tubing rather than rigid copper, because it flexes around framing without needing as many joints, resists the mineral scale buildup that's common with San Diego County's hard water, and holds up well over decades of typical residential use.

Timing a Repipe Around Other Home Projects

If a kitchen or bathroom remodel is already planned, that's often the most efficient time to repipe the section of the house being opened up anyway, access is already available, and the two projects share some of the same demolition and patching work. Coordinating timing with a planned remodel can meaningfully reduce the total disruption compared to doing the repipe as a separate, later project.

Permits and Inspection

Whole-home repiping in California typically requires a permit and inspection, since it involves the structural water supply system rather than a like-for-like fixture swap. That process is handled as part of the job rather than left for the homeowner to navigate separately.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a whole-home repipe take?+

It depends on the home's size and how accessible the existing lines are, but most residential repipes are completed over a few days rather than weeks. An exact timeline is part of the quote after the inspection.

Will I have water during the repipe?+

Water is typically shut off in sections as work progresses rather than for the entire duration, the crew will walk through exactly what to expect for your specific home before starting.

Is repiping covered by homeowners insurance?+

Coverage varies by policy and by whether the repipe is preventive or in response to actual damage, that's a conversation with your insurance provider, not something we can promise either way.
