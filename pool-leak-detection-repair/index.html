Pool Leak Detection & Repair in San Marcos, CA

A pool that's losing more water than a hot North County summer accounts for usually has a real leak somewhere in the plumbing, the shell, or the equipment pad, rather than simple evaporation. With San Elijo Hills, Lake San Marcos, and other newer San Marcos communities carrying a meaningful share of pool-equipped homes, this is a genuinely common call rather than a rare specialty request.

How We Diagnose the Problem

The first step is confirming there's an actual leak rather than normal evaporation, using a bucket test: fill a bucket with pool water, set it on a step so the water levels match, and compare the drop in both after 24 hours. If the pool drops noticeably more than the bucket, that's a real leak, not just summer evaporation. From there, dye testing at suspected fitting locations and pressure testing the underground plumbing lines narrow down whether the leak is in the shell, a skimmer, a light niche, or a buried pipe run between the pool and the equipment pad.

Signs You Need This Service

  • Water level dropping noticeably faster than a bucket test accounts for
  • A consistently running pool pump that seems to be adding water more than usual
  • Wet or unusually soggy ground near the pool equipment pad or along a plumbing run
  • A cracked or visibly damaged pool light niche or skimmer housing
  • Air bubbles coming from a return jet during normal operation

Pool Leak Detection & Repair Pricing & What Affects It

A leak isolated to an accessible fitting or a skimmer is a relatively contained repair. A leak in a buried plumbing line running under decking or hardscape is a larger job, and the diagnostic step, confirming exactly which line and where along its run, is what keeps that repair from turning into unnecessary excavation across the whole yard.

Bucket Test vs. Professional Leak Detection

The bucket test is a useful first step any homeowner can do, but it only confirms that a leak exists, not where it is. Professional detection combines dye testing at suspected fittings, pressure testing isolated sections of plumbing, and sometimes underwater camera inspection to find the specific location, which is what actually determines whether the fix is a simple fitting repair or a more involved plumbing line repair.

Shell Leaks vs. Plumbing Leaks

A leak in the pool's shell itself, a crack in plaster or a separated tile line, behaves differently from a leak in the underground plumbing connecting the pool to its equipment. Shell leaks often show a water level that stabilizes at a specific point (wherever the crack sits below the waterline), while plumbing leaks can drain the pool further depending on which line is affected and whether it's a pressure-side or suction-side leak.

Equipment Pad Leaks Are Often the Easiest Fix

Leaks at the equipment pad itself, a loose union fitting, a cracked pump housing, or a failed O-ring, are typically the most straightforward to find and repair since everything is visible and accessible, unlike underground plumbing. A thorough visual inspection of the equipment pad is a reasonable first step before assuming a leak requires more invasive underground detection.

Why Pool Leaks Matter Beyond the Water Bill

Beyond wasted water, a sustained pool leak can undermine surrounding decking or hardscape if the water is saturating soil beneath it, and in some cases can affect nearby structures if the leak is significant and long-running. Catching a leak early through routine bucket testing, especially during peak summer use when evaporation makes changes harder to notice, avoids this kind of secondary damage.

Pressure-Side vs. Suction-Side Leaks

Leaks on the pressure side of the pump, the plumbing carrying water back to the pool after it's filtered, tend to show visible wet spots along the line's path since water is being pushed out under pressure. Suction-side leaks, on the plumbing pulling water from the pool into the equipment, often show no visible wet spot at all and instead pull in air, which shows up as bubbles at return jets or a pump that struggles to maintain prime. Identifying which type is present changes both the testing approach and the eventual repair.

Pool Leaks in North County Backyards

Pools are common in North County backyards, including larger lots in Twin Oaks Valley and communities around Lake San Marcos and Carlsbad's La Costa. Our work covers plumbing leaks and not shell or plaster repairs.

A bucket test confirms whether you have a real leak before any equipment arrives. After that we check the equipment pad, then the buried lines.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my pool actually has a leak or if it's just evaporation?+

A bucket test is the standard first check: compare the water level drop in the pool against a bucket of pool water over 24 hours. If the pool drops meaningfully more than the bucket, that points to a real leak.

Can a pool leak be fixed without draining the whole pool?+

Often yes, especially for equipment pad leaks or leaks accessible without disturbing the shell, though the specific repair location determines whether any draining is needed.

Do you repair the pool shell itself, or just the plumbing?+

This covers plumbing-related leaks, supply and return lines, equipment pad fittings, and related plumbing components. Shell and plaster repairs are typically handled by a pool specialist rather than a plumber.

Can you check a spa or hot tub leak?+

Describe the equipment and where you see water when you call. The plumber can tell you whether the issue falls within pool and spa plumbing or needs a different specialist.
